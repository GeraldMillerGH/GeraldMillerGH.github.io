Fire Pork

A riff from Cookish

Servings: 4  ·  Source: The Lazy Genius Newsletter, ~June 2025

Ingredients

  • 2 pork tenderloins
  • 3 tbsp sriracha
  • 3 tbsp mirin (honey or sugar can sub, but cut to ~2 tbsp — mirin is best)
  • 4–5 tbsp soy sauce
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Cooked rice, for serving

Quick steps

  1. Combine sriracha, mirin, and soy sauce in a bowl.
  2. Slice pork tenderloin into thinnish (~½") medallions.
  3. Season pork lightly with salt and pepper.
  4. Reserve 1–2 tbsp of marinade; pour the rest over the pork in a bag or bowl. Marinate 1–8 hours.
  5. Pull pork from the marinade and cook on a grill, cast iron skillet, or stovetop, 1–2 minutes per side, until just cooked through.
  6. Serve over rice, drizzled with the reserved marinade. Add a vegetable or green onion if you want.
Detailed instructions
  1. Combine the sriracha, mirin, and soy in a glass measuring cup or bowl.
  2. Slice the pork tenderloin into thinnish medallions — maybe half an inch thick, no need to be precise.
  3. Season the pork lightly with a few pinches of salt and some black pepper. The soy will add more seasoning as it marinates.
  4. Pour all but 1–2 tablespoons of the marinade into a plastic gallon bag or large bowl (save the rest for later). Add the pork and marinate for 1–8 hours — more time means more flavor, but even 30 minutes works fine.
  5. Pull the pork pieces out of the liquid (there won't be much liquid left) and cook on a grill, in a cast iron skillet on a grill, or straight on the stove. Leave a little space between pieces so they sauté rather than steam. Cook about a minute or two per side until you get a little color — pork tenderloin is naturally tender, so it cooks fast.
  6. Serve the cooked pork over rice with a drizzle of the reserved marinade (not the marinade from the pork bag — that needs to be boiled separately first). Add a vegetable or some green onion if you like.

Notes

  • If your grill runs low on heat, the pork may steam a bit instead of sautéing — it'll still turn out well.
  • A little smoke on the stovetop is normal.
